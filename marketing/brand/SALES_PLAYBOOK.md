## 1. Category — what SignalSync IS

**We are not** a web design company, an SEO agency, or a marketing agency.
**We are a Customer Capture System for contractors.**

One sentence: *"Marketing agencies make you look good. SignalSync makes sure the
customer who's already looking for you actually reaches you — and books you."*

**The enemy:** the leak. Every contractor's town already produces enough demand —
searches, calls, quote requests. The problem is leakage: searches they don't appear
in, calls that hit voicemail, quote requests that go to whoever answered first,
great work that never becomes a review. We don't "generate demand." We capture the
demand that already exists and is currently leaking to competitors.

**Why existing options fail:**
- *Agencies* sell activity (posts, reports, impressions) — not captured jobs — on
  12-month contracts that outlive their motivation.
- *Freelancers* build a website and vanish. Nobody watches the phone, the reviews,
  or the rankings afterward.
- *Lead marketplaces* (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack) sell the SAME lead to 3–5 of
  your competitors and own the customer relationship forever. You're renting.
- *Doing nothing* works until the competitor down the road stops doing nothing.

## 3. Cold call scripts

### Opening (first 10 seconds — earn the next 30)
> "Hey, is this [First name]? ... [Name], I'll be quick because I know you're
> probably on a job. My name's Will — I run a small company that finds jobs
> contractors are losing without knowing it. I pulled up [Business] before I
> called and found a couple things that are probably costing you work. You got
> 60 seconds or should I call back after 5?"

Why it works: permission + respect for their time + a specific claim about THEIR
business (never generic) + an easy out that paradoxically keeps them on.

**Rules:** call from the (415) number, stand up, smile, talk slower than feels
natural. If they say "after 5" — actually call at 5:01. That alone separates you
from every telemarketer they've ever hung up on.

### Discovery questions (listen 80%, talk 20%)
1. "When someone calls while you're up on a roof / running the machine — what
   happens to that call?"
2. "You know roughly how many calls you miss in a week? Most guys I talk to guess
   low by half."
3. "When a homeowner searches '[their trade] near me' in [their town] — you know
   who comes up first? Have you ever actually looked?"
4. "How do you get reviews right now — do you ask, or just hope?"
5. "Where does most of your work come from today — and what happens if that dries
   up for a month?"
6. (The money question) "What's the biggest thing slowing down new business right
   now?" — then SHUT UP and write down their exact words. Their answer is the
   pitch.

### The 60-second explanation
> "Here's the whole thing. People in [town] are already searching for
> [trade] — tonight, this weekend. Right now some of those people can't find
> you, and some who DO find you can't reach you — no website, or the call goes
> to voicemail and they just dial the next name. I fix that leak. You show up
> when they search, they can request a quote at 9pm from their couch, missed
> calls get an instant text back so they don't call your competitor, and after
> every job your customer gets a text asking for a review — so your rating
> starts matching your work. You keep running your crew. I send you a plain
> report every month: calls, quotes, reviews, where you rank. Month to month,
> no contract — if your phone doesn't ring more, fire me."

### The proof move (do this on every warm call)
While on the phone: "You near your truck? Google '[their trade] [their town]'
right now. I'll wait." Let them SEE themselves missing / competitor first.
The audit does this in writing; live is 10x stronger.

### Follow-ups
- **No answer:** voicemail ≤15 sec: "Will here — found a couple things costing
  [Business] jobs, nothing urgent, I'll text you the short version." Then text
  the audit link. (Their number is in the CRM; the platform drafted it.)
- **Interest, then silence (3 days):** "Hey [Name], Will. Not chasing you — just
  didn't want you to think I forgot. That report on [Business] is still yours
  either way. Worth 10 minutes this week?"
- **After objection:** log the objection in the CRM interaction notes, wait the
  interval they implied, come back with NEW information ("that competitor I
  mentioned just passed you in reviews"), never the same pitch again.
- **After proposal (48h):** "Only question I have: is it the money, the timing,
  or you're just not sure it'll work? All three have honest answers."

## 4. Objection destroyer

Format: what they mean → the real barrier → response → follow-up question.

**"Too expensive."**
Mean: "I don't see the return." Barrier: price framed as cost, not leak.
> "Fair. Let me frame it different — what's an average job worth to you? [They
> say $800–$5k+.] So if this catches you ONE job a month it pays for itself,
> and everything after that's profit. The audit shows exactly where jobs are
> leaking — want to at least see the leak before deciding what it's worth
> plugging?"
Follow-up: "What's an average job run for you?"

**"I don't need marketing."**
Mean: "Marketing = ads and fluff, I'm a word-of-mouth guy." Barrier: category
confusion — agree with him.
> "You're right, and I'm not selling marketing. Word of mouth is the best lead
> there is. I just make sure that when someone hears your name and looks you
> up — or searches when nobody gave them a name — they find YOU and reach YOU.
> That's not marketing, that's answering the phone."
Follow-up: "When someone gets referred to you, what do they do first — call, or
look you up?"

**"I can do this myself."**
Mean: "I don't want to depend on anyone." Barrier: pride + past DIY.
> "You probably could — none of this is rocket science. It's just hours. Same
> reason your customers COULD rent the equipment and don't. Question is whether
> your best hours are better spent on this or on the work that pays."
Follow-up: "When's the last time you had a free evening to mess with Google?"

**"I don't want another monthly payment."**
Mean: "I've been trapped by subscriptions/contracts." Barrier: commitment fear.
> "Neither do I — which is why there's no contract. Month to month, cancel with
> an email. I have to re-earn it every 30 days, which is exactly how you keep a
> vendor honest."
Follow-up: "What burned you before — a contract you couldn't get out of?"

**"I've been burned before." / "Marketing companies never work."**
Mean: "I paid, saw a report full of jargon, nothing changed." Barrier: trust debt
someone else created. DO NOT defend the industry.
> "You probably got sold impressions and 'engagement.' I don't report those. My
> monthly report is: calls, quote requests, reviews, where you rank in [town].
> Numbers you can check yourself. And month one is judged on the free audit —
> which you get whether you hire me or not."
Follow-up: "What did the last company actually promise you?"

**"How do I know this gets results?"**
Mean: reasonable skepticism — reward it.
> "You don't yet, and you shouldn't take my word. That's why it starts with the
> free written audit — everything in it is measured, nothing invented. If the
> problems in it aren't real, you'll know in 5 minutes and we're done. If they
> are real, that's your answer."

**"Call me later." / "We're busy."**
Mean: often true in the trades (seasonal). Barrier: none — respect it, anchor it.
> "Good problem. One thing though: busy season is exactly when your missed-call
> leak is biggest — every voicemail right now is a customer calling the next guy.
> How about I send the audit today, you skim it in 5 minutes, and I call you
> [specific day]?"
Follow-up: "Morning or after 5 on [day]?"

**"Maybe next year."**
> "That's fine — reviews and rankings compound though, like interest. The guy who
> starts this year is ahead of the guy who starts next year, forever. Can I at
> least leave you the audit so you know where you stand?"

**"I'm not tech savvy."**
Mean: fear of becoming tech support's hostage. Barrier: competence anxiety.
> "Perfect, because you never touch any of it. You run the crew, I run this. The
> only tech skill you need is reading a text message — you've got that."

**"My customers just call."**
> "Exactly — the phone IS the business. This whole system exists to make it ring
> more and make sure nothing's lost when you can't grab it. I'm not changing how
> you get work, I'm patching the holes in it."

**"We already have referrals."**
> "Then you're sitting on free money — referred customers still look you up
> before calling, and right now some of them see [the actual measured problem:
> 4 reviews / no site / broken site]. Referrals PLUS showing up right is how the
> biggest guy in town got to be the biggest guy in town."

**"We're already number one."**
Mean: maybe true! Verify before the call — if they ARE strong, say so.
> "You might be — you're one of maybe two in [town] doing this right, which is
> exactly why I'd want you as the case study. Staying number one is cheaper than
> getting there. Want to see what the guy in second place would need to do to
> pass you? It's in the audit."

## 5. Competitive moat — the mechanism they can't copy

Our unique mechanism, in customer language:
**"We audit before we sell, we measure everything we claim, and you can fire us
monthly."**

Under the hood (never pitch this, it just makes the above true at scale): the
platform continuously scans every contractor in a market, measures 8 factors,
scores the opportunity, generates the evidence-based audit, and tracks every
promise against measured results. An agency does this manually for prospects
one at a time; we do it for entire states before breakfast. Freelancers can't
watch; marketplaces won't — their business model IS the leak (reselling the
same customer five times).

Positioning one-liners per competitor:
- vs agencies: "They send reports about impressions. We send reports about calls."
- vs freelancers: "A website nobody maintains is a truck nobody oils."
- vs Angi/HomeAdvisor: "They rent you a lead they also rented to three competitors.
  We make YOU the one who owns the customer."
- vs DIY: "You could. It's just hours — and your hours have a rate."

   makes "fire us if numbers don't move" auditable. (New service + cron + template.)

   number per client, missed-call webhook → instant SMS). Highest perceived-value
   service becomes software we own. (New `services/textback.py`, Twilio env, client
   provisioning table.)
4. **Review engine module** — post-job review-request texts with client job feed
   (manual entry v1). (Twilio shared infra from #3.)
5. **Outscraper enrichment** — emails + socials for the backlog → unlocks email
   sequences at scale. (Already scoped: CSV import adapter exists.)
6. **Massachusetts + expansion states** — after CT calls prove the pitch.
Rule unchanged since day one: no feature ships before the previous one is used
in real selling.
